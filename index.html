Contract Ledger

What if you read an NBA team like a balance sheet? Contracts are what a front office spends; production is what it gets back. This ledger prices every 2026-27 contract against what the player actually produces, to see which teams spend their money best.

  1. Price a win. A replacement-level player costs the league minimum ($1.36M). Every Win Share above replacement is worth $6.20M, the rate at which total value matches total league salary.
  2. Value each player. The minimum plus $6.20M for each Win Share above replacement is what his production is worth. Small samples are pulled toward average, and bench bigs get a rebounding discount. Subtract his salary and you get surplus: positive is a bargain, negative is overpaid.
  3. Add up each team. Sum the surplus on a roster and subtract dead money (salary still owed to players who are gone) to rank all 30 front offices.

Click any dot, row or team to dig in. Built by Sreekar as a weekend project; data from NBA.com, Basketball Reference and Spotrac.

Players under 500 minutes last season are left out unless they have 2026-27 minutes, in which case they are valued on this season alone and flagged as small samples.

"If healthy" projects each player to all 82 games at his own minutes per game. "With 2026-27" pools both seasons by minutes, so it barely moves until December. Rookie deals are first-round picks from the 2023-2026 drafts, whose pay is set by the league's rookie scale. VORP is shown as a second opinion; it ranks players much like Win Shares (rank correlation 0.83) but rates high-usage creators higher.
