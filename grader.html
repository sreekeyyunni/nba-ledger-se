How a grade is set. Each team's grade is the trade value it takes in minus the trade value it sends out. A player's trade value is the Contract Ledger's number: every remaining contract year, projected Win Shares with an aging curve times a price per Win Share that grows 7% a year, minus his salary, with later years discounted 8% a year. A cheap, productive contract is worth a lot; an overpaid one is worth less than nothing, so dumping it counts as a gain. Play-style fit then moves the grade: each point of fit (−10 to +10, from NBA.com play-type and tracking data) counts like $1.5M of trade value, so a deal that fills a team's holes grades better than one that doubles up on what it already has. Playoff odds, title odds and projected wins come from the Season sim, which plays out the rest of the season 2,000 times with and without the deal (for a trade that already happened, "before" puts the players back). They show what the deal does to this season, not the grade.

What it can't see. Grades measure contract value, not talent alone. A star on a max deal is worth about what he's paid here, so trading him for good-value role players can grade as a win for the team getting the role players. Draft picks use the Ledger's pick values (2027-2033, with each team's finish simulated and existing protections and swaps priced in). A pick the Ledger doesn't list, a new swap, or a protection the deal adds gets a rough adjustment, and the deal table marks it as an estimate. Players missing from the tracker (two-way and minimum deals, recent signings) count as $0 until you type their salary.

Salary rules. 2023 CBA Article VII 6(j) and 2(e), with the 2026-27 cap at $164.96M, tax $200.43M, aprons $209.02M and $221.69M. Payroll is Spotrac total cash, so teams right at an apron can differ from the official number. Not checked: cash, existing trade exceptions, sign-and-trades, and the re-trade waiting periods.
