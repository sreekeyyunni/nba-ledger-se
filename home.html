How the Contract Ledger works

Read an NBA team like a balance sheet: contracts are what a front office spends, production is what it gets back. The Ledger prices every 2026-27 contract against what the player produces.

  1. Price a win. A replacement-level player costs the league minimum ($1.36M). Every Win Share above replacement is worth $6.20M, the rate at which total value matches total league salary.
  2. Value each player. The minimum plus $6.20M per Win Share above replacement is what his production is worth. Subtract his salary and you get surplus: positive is a bargain, negative is overpaid.
  3. Credit the stars. Win Shares undersell high-usage stars, so players who made All-NBA in the last two seasons or rank top 10 in VORP get 75% of any overpay forgiven, and the rest of the top 25 get 40%.
  4. Count injury time. When a report gives a timeline ("out two months"), the games he'll miss come off his 2026-27 value, his trade value and the season sim. "If healthy" ignores it.
  5. Add up each team. Sum the surplus on a roster and subtract dead money (salary owed to players who are gone) to rank all 30 front offices.
